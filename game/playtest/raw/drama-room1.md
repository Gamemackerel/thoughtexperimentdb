# drama · room 1 (house, trolley-problem, brain-in-a-vat, platos-cave, ship-of-theseus)

## Summary
- The raw material is strong. Every vignette has at least one moment where the drama comes straight from the source text: the one worker's slowed-down "They always put me on my own…", the cave prisoners' "I've counted. The bird always comes after the jar.", the fisherman's grandfather's rod, the lab reveal with "you" still sitting on the bench above, and the third track turning the lever back on you.
- The narration often gets out of step with what the player does. Lines are queued and timed rather than tied to actions, so the setup arrives after the choice and the reflection arrives after the ending. The trolley's question comes after you've already pulled the lever. The cave's "stay or go back?" plays after "You stay in the light". The vat's "All of it feels solid" plays after you've sat down. This happens in three of four vignettes, and it breaks the setup → choice → consequence order that the whole design relies on.
- Endings can be reached before the dilemma has been set up. You can sit on the vat bench 3 s after arriving, and you can sit under the cave tree before the choice is voiced. The closing lines then answer a doubt the player never had ("Real or not, it's the only sun you have").
- The trolley's best sting ("the one … was never in danger, until you moved the trolley") is replaced by the replay line on every run after the first. Most players will hear it once at most, and players who wait on run 1 never hear it at all.
- Ship of Theseus leans toward one answer. The new-ship ending is a confident verdict ("Its story didn't [change]"), while the old-wood ending is a doubtful question. Stopping halfway also does nothing at all, so "doing nothing" isn't a real option there.
- The consequence and reflection beats are short everywhere: about 1.6 s of aftermath on the trolley, about 2 s of the final fall in the vat, and about 7 s of sailing before the card covers the ship.

## Findings

### trolley-problem
- **[major] [narrative/pacing]** The setup lines play on a timer, not when you discover things. If you stay at spawn, the lever line ("This lever switches the tracks.") never plays, so "Should you pull it?" has no "it" in the voice-over. If you rush to the lever instead (walked from spawn and pulled at t≈173), you pull and put it back before the narrator has introduced the five, the one or the question. Those lines arrived at 181, 186 and 189 s, after I had already acted. The *discover* beat is missing either way: the camera shows the five and the one from the very first frame (shot `003-t12`/`004-t15`), and the proximity triggers at (14.5, 0) and (14, -7) show "not fired" while their lines still play.
  Suggestion: hold "slow" until the lever line has played. Voice "five/one" when the player actually walks toward them, or at least before any lever interaction is allowed. Make "ask" wait for "lever". If the player pulls before "ask", drop "ask" and go straight to the silence.
- **[major] [narrative]** The pulled_2 sting is lost on replays. On run 2 I pulled the lever and heard "You pulled the lever. The five are safe." followed by "This time you chose the other way. Most people feel the pull of both." The key reflection ("But the one on the side track was never in danger, until you moved the trolley.") was replaced. A player who waits on run 1 and pulls on run 2 (the most natural order) never hears the doing/allowing line at all.
  Suggestion: always play the second reflection line for the route taken, and add the again_same/again_diff line after it (or fold it in: "This time you pulled. And the one was never in danger, until you did.").
- **[minor] [narrative]** again_diff repeats word for word. Runs 2 (pulled) and 3 (waited) both ended with "This time you chose the other way. Most people feel the pull of both." (t=117 and t=154). The third time it sounds canned, and "most people feel the pull of both" is a lecture-ish generalisation to hear twice.
  Suggestion: write a run-3 variant ("Back and forth. The lever doesn't get any lighter.") or only acknowledge the switch once.
- **[major] [narrative/philosophy]** The closing line contradicts the game's own twist. The three-runs "end" line is "Whatever you choose, someone is standing on the track. You only decide who." The card says "Nobody could stop the trolley. You only decided where it went." But from run 2 onward the game has offered a lever that puts *you* on the track and spares everyone else. For a player who saw the twist and declined it, the line should acknowledge that refusal. That refusal is the actual point of the third track: if you wouldn't take it yourself, may you send it to the one?
  Suggestion: when twist has been shown and not taken, end with something like "You could have stood there yourself. You didn't. Neither did the one get a say." Keep the current line for players who never saw the third track.
- **[minor] [pacing]** The consequence doesn't hold. From "go" to aftermath to rewind took about 5 s + 1.6 s (e.g. 104.5 go, 109.8 aftermath, 111.4 rewind). The impact shot frames the victim well (shot `013-t110`), but it never shows *you*, the chooser, next to what you caused. It rewinds before the weight lands.
  Suggestion: hold the aftermath for 3–4 s. End it with a cut or pull-back to a two-shot of the player and the knocked-over worker(s) before the rewind.
- **[minor] [agency]** "Go again, or step back through the frame." is followed by an automatic restart 3.5 s later (82.6 → 86.1 s). The line offers a choice that the game then makes for you, and the next run starts while you're still taking in the twist.
  Suggestion: start the next run only once the player moves toward a lever or the track, or after a much longer silence (15 s or more).
- **[minor] [narrative]** The narrator doesn't notice wavering. Pulling the lever and then putting it back (t=173 → 177) got no response. Hesitation is the most dramatic thing a player can do here.
  Suggestion: add one line for a player who switches back, e.g. "You put it back. For now."
- **[idea] [drama]** The journal question ("…wouldn't push someone off a bridge…") introduces the footbridge case, which is the most famous turn in this literature, only as homework. It's the natural second act: after the three runs, a footbridge with a large stranger appears over the track.
  Suggestion: offer it as a fourth-run variant (with the same gentle, toy-like physics), or at least have the narrator set it up in the final line so the card question doesn't come out of nowhere.
- **[polish] [narrative]** The self-sacrifice ending plays well (shots `019-t263`, `021-t267`, "You chose yourself"). But it arrives only one run in, and it's the only ending where nobody else is hurt, so martyrdom reads as the "solution".
  Suggestion: in the self_end card line, keep some doubt about it being the solution (e.g. "…The trolley only needed someone on the track. You decided it had to be you.").
- **[polish] [clarity]** The notebook (N) doesn't pause the scene. I opened it during "slow", and the trolley kept creeping and the lines kept playing underneath. It also says "This short film walks through…", which is film copy shown inside the game.
  Suggestion: pause the level's clock while the notebook is open, and replace "This short film" with game-neutral copy.

### brain-in-a-vat
- **[major] [narrative/structure]** You can reach the bench ending before any doubt has been raised. I pressed "Sit down" at t=425, 3 s after arriving. The queued "solid" line then played after I was seated, and the ending said "Real or not, it's the only sun you have." and "You never found out whether the sun was real. You felt it anyway." The player never wondered, so there's nothing to "never find out". Choosing ignorance only means something after the doubt has appeared.
  Suggestion: enable "Sit down" only after `edgeSeen` (or after the tree/window/edge line has played). Or give an early sit its own ending ("You sit down. It never occurs to you to wonder.") with a different card.
- **[minor] [pacing]** The escalation stalls on layer 2. After "Another brain. Another machine. Another world above it." there were about 20 s of silence with nothing new (t=382 → 402), then the same line as layer 1 ("Here, too, the edge flickers."). The third fall ends only 2.2 s later with no third reveal (phase over at 412.4).
  Suggestion: make layer 2 strange in a new way, e.g. the machine is labelled with the same words as your first world, or the vat on the layer above has a second tiny "you" on a bench. Give lab_edge a layer-2 variant ("It flickers here too. Of course it does."). Show one more beat of the fall (a longer pull-back through all the nested worlds) before "However far you climb out…".
- **[minor] [narrative]** The final line's metaphor doesn't match the action. The end line says "However far you climb out…", but the action every time is stepping *off* an edge and falling.
  Suggestion: use "However far you step out…" or "However many times you fall outward…".
- **[minor] [narrative]** Your own body is left unremarked. The lab reveal shows the teal "you" still sitting on the bench in the world above (the `sitter` in code, visible in shot `028-t339`). It's the most unsettling image in the vignette, and the narrator walks past it.
  Suggestion: add one short line ("Up there, someone who looks like you is still sitting on the bench."), or a "Look up" aside in layer 1.
- **[idea] [drama]** There's no way to stay in the lab. Once you're outside, the only verb left is "step off" again. Choosing to accept the lab as the real world ("So this is what's real. Isn't it?") would be a strong third ending, and a dramatic mirror of the bench.
  Suggestion: add a stool by the machine in the lab ("Sit by the machine") that ends with the lab treated as real, e.g. "You decided this one was the bottom. You can't check that either."
- **[idea] [philosophy/drama]** The vignette dramatises Cartesian regress. The journal to-do says the source's actual twist is that you "can't even coherently say it", and nothing in play touches that.
  Suggestion: in the lab layers, the machine's labels or the player's words could be blank or flicker whenever the player looks at "brain" or "vat" (e.g. the machine's screen reads "tree" where the tree is), so the player feels that the words were only ever about the simulated things.

### platos-cave
- **[major] [narrative/ordering]** The choice line is voiced after the choice has been made. Played briskly (walked straight out, used "Sit under the tree" at t=681.8), the order was: "A path leads up, towards a light." (played outside, t=674) → "It's too bright…" → "Outside. The real things…" → *sat* → "You stay in the light." (684.5) → "You could stay up here. Or go back down, and tell them." (686.6) → card. Also, "Sit under the tree" is enabled before `saidChoose`, while "Go back down" correctly waits for it.
  Suggestion: gate "Sit under the tree" on `S.saidChoose` like "Go back down" is. Flush or cancel queued arrival lines when the player changes place (cave → out) or reaches an ending.
- **[major] [philosophy/drama]** The ascent skips the pain that makes the allegory dramatic. Chains fall by themselves on a timer (t≈465), and the turn line gives an instant disillusion ("The shadows were only this."). "It's too bright to look at" is followed by daylight about 1 s later. In the source, the prisoner is compelled, is dazzled, wants to turn back, and thinks the shadows are truer. That resistance is exactly what makes "keep watching" a meaningful choice.
  Suggestion: after turning to the fire, keep the fire too bright to approach at first (walking toward it slows you and whites out the view), with a line like "It hurts to look. The wall was easier." Let the path out be slow, with a white-out, and hold the player in the pond/reflections stage before the sun: "Look in the pond" already exists and should be a step on the way up, not an optional aside.
- **[minor] [narrative]** The keep-watching line presumes things the player didn't do. I never turned my head, then chose "Sit back down" right after "Your chains have come loose." (t=638). I heard "You turn back to the wall. The shapes are familiar. It's easier." But I never turned away, and "easier" implies I knew what I was refusing. The journal question ("What was it like to turn around, and did you try to tell anyone?") fits only the "went back" ending.
  Suggestion: add a variant for a player who never looked behind ("You never looked round. The shapes are all there is."). Rephrase the journal question so it works for all three endings.
- **[minor] [drama]** The return is only narrated. "Tell them" is a single E press, then "They laugh. The climb has ruined your eyes, they say. Nobody else will go." (t=569). In shot `064-t572` the prisoners just stand still, and the card follows 6 s later. The confrontation, which is the allegory's political climax, happens entirely off-screen.
  Suggestion: let the prisoners turn their heads toward you and give each one a speech bubble of ridicule (the talk system with `BACK[]` lines already exists). Delay the card until they've turned back to the wall.
- **[polish] [discoverability]** The best asides are hidden. The prisoners' prediction talk ("Shh. The horse is next.", "I've counted. The bird always comes after the jar.") only appears if you turn your head while chained, and nothing suggests you can. This is Plato's honours-for-predicting-shadows detail, beautifully done.
  Suggestion: have one prisoner speak unprompted once during the chained phase, so the player learns they're there.

### ship-of-theseus
- **[major] [narrative/bias]** The two endings are not treated equally. New ship: "You chose the ship that kept sailing. Its parts changed. Its story didn't." with the card "…it never stopped being the ship that sailed." Old wood: "You chose the original wood. Every plank is the same. But is the ship?" with the card "Whether that makes it the same ship is the question." One choice gets a verdict and the other gets doubt, which goes against "never tell the player they were right or wrong".
  Suggestion: give both endings the same shape: a plain description, then a doubt. For example, new: "You chose the ship that kept sailing. Not one plank of it was there at the start. Is it?" Old: "You chose the original wood. Every plank was there at the start. Its voyages weren't."
- **[major] [structure]** Doing nothing is not an option, and the choice has no consequence or replay. I replaced 3 planks and then waited 45 s (t=870 → 915) and got nothing: no line, no ending. After boarding, the card appears about 7 s into the sail, with no consequence, no rewind and no replay acknowledgement. The template's twist and replay beats are missing.
  Suggestion: at half-done, add a line if the player stops ("Half old, half new. Which half is the ship?"). After the choice, let the other ship do something (the builder sails it the other way, or both ships reach the same harbour and are both called by the same name). On a replay, have the narrator note same or different.
- **[major] [interaction]** An irreversible choice can be made by accident. In the choose phase, "Talk" (the builder at 12.5, -1.1) and "Board the ship of new planks" (16, -0.9) are close together, and board takes priority. Pressing E next to the builder boarded the new ship (t=826.5), and the vignette ended on a choice I didn't make.
  Suggestion: move the board prompts to the ships' gangplanks, away from the builder. Or require walking onto the ship (a trigger) instead of E, which is also more "act, don't answer".
- **[minor] [pacing]** Replays are a chore. A replay repeats all 12 plank trips (about 60 s of fetch and carry) before you reach the only new decision.
  Suggestion: on replay, start with the ship already half-replaced (or let one E hold carry several planks), and keep the full chore for the first time.
- **[minor] [bug]** A speech bubble stays stuck. The fisherman's "Mind you, they have replaced every board of it since." stayed on screen from t≈785 through the rebuild, the choice and the game-over card (visible in shots `071`, `072`, `073`).
  Suggestion: fade talk bubbles after their normal duration, and clear them on phase changes and on gameOver.
- **[polish] [narrative]** "The ship of Theseus." is the first line. It's the only proper name in the room-1 narration, which goes against pillar 4.
  Suggestion: "An old ship. Its wood is getting old." / "Two ships. Which one is the ship?"

### house
- **[minor] [narrative]** The journal overwrites the ending. It stores one ending per vignette: after the new-ship run (answered) and then the old-wood run, it shows "You sailed the old wood" next to my answer, which argued for the repaired ship. The record of what I did and the reflection I wrote no longer match.
  Suggestion: store the ending with each answer (or keep a short list of endings reached: "You sailed the new ship · You sailed the old wood").

## Keep
- The one worker's slowed, stretched speech ("Theeeyyy aaalwaaayyys puuut meee ooon myyy ooown…"). It humanises the one without making it heavy.
- The third track and self-lever, and its line "If not, is it fair to send it to someone else?". It's real theory turned into a playable twist.
- The vat's asides: "Every leaf exactly where you'd expect it", "you've never seen anyone go in", "Somewhere in here is that tree". They seed doubt without lecturing.
- The lab reveal with the whole world floating over the jar, and the "you" left on the bench.
- The cave's first-person chained view and the prisoners' prediction talk. "Down here, you can barely see anymore." on return. "Your reflection. Another kind of shadow, but this one looks back."
- The fisherman's grandad's rod, and the builder's "Seemed a shame to burn them." / "Which one would you sail?"
- The trolley's game-over title "Someone is always on the track" and the vat's "You can only ever check the world with the world."
