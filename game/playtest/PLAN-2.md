# Work list, 27 September (second round)

From your message after the audit, and the new notes in `feedback.txt` (Ship of Theseus, Brain in a Vat, Footbridge, the
Loop, the Trolley Problem, Transplant, Paperclip). Done in this order; each step is played through, then committed.

## 1. Everywhere
- [ ] **Same every time.** No narration or content that depends on having played before: drop the shared "last time you
      chose the same / differently" lines, and every vignette's replay branch (the Experience Machine's "you've been
      plugged in all along" twist goes; the notebook still describes it).
- [ ] **Asides stop covering their own bubbles.** Once you use a talk or look prompt, that prompt hides for a few seconds.

## 2. The first room
- [ ] The sky door's artifact (the blocky, see-through overlay).
- [ ] The window in the floor becomes a real hole: you look down a shaft into the sky, clouds drifting at different
      depths below (parallax), instead of a flat picture. No prompt on it.
- [ ] No narration for the room's things. Instead something happens: the Penrose climber falls down a whole flight (and
      picks himself up); the melting clock melts further, drips all the way down into a puddle on the floor, then reforms.

## 3. Rooms
- [ ] Gallery: the painter's easel shows a different painting from the one on the wall.
- [ ] Gallery: the Prisoner's Dilemma is greyed out (closed; the cell door can't be opened).

## 4. Small vignette fixes
- [ ] **Brain in a Vat:** a line early on about how the world exists at all, the nature of reality; the edge line becomes
      "By some incredible glitch, or magic, you've glimpsed the true nature of reality. Something flickers at the edge of
      your vision. Will you go after it, and try to see what's beneath?"

- [ ] **Ship of Theseus:** "someone decided to use all the old parts" (not "kept"); no replay acknowledgement.

## 5. Newcomb's Paradox
- [ ] Say plainly: the money is already in the room, and nothing you do now can change what's in the boxes; and every
      visitor who took both boxes found the closed one empty (the ledger shows it).
- [ ] Take both on day one: it ends. Take one: come back tomorrow and both boxes are glass. You can see the million.

## 6. The trolleys
- [ ] **Trolley Problem:** no frame to step back through; after the run with the new switch, it doesn't run again.
- [ ] **Footbridge:** the trolley animated like the first one's; one run, no rewind; he doesn't fall through the bridge;
      a struggle when you push him.
- [ ] **The Loop:** the same trolley; one quick sentence up front (the big man would stop the trolley, but only if you
      steer it into him; otherwise the five would stop it, and he'd be fine); a quick ghost run of each option; then the
      real run once, and a very short line on what happened.

## 7. The Gettier cases: act on the belief
- [ ] **Ten Coins:** bet on it (and win, for the wrong reason).
- [ ] **Stopped Clock:** no choice (the postman goes): you read the clock and set off for the 2:15 train, and make it;
      then day turns to night and back to day, and the clock never moves.
- [ ] **Sheep in the Field:** shut the gate to keep the sheep in; the gate keeps in a sheep you never saw.
- [ ] **Barn Façades:** a storm's coming; run for the barn's shelter; it's the only one with anything behind it.
- [ ] **Mirage:** call your companion over, and lead them to the water.

## 8. Paperclip Maximiser (from the feedback box)
- [ ] Context: your business is paperclips, and there's metal to use.
- [ ] Only "as many as you can" at first. After that runs its course, a rewind, and a new button: "exactly one hundred".
- [ ] The hundred: it's the word *exactly*. What counts as a paperclip; 99.9999 per cent sure isn't sure. Say
      "alignment" out loud: we know what we meant, but can it? Its copies and factories aren't making more clips; they're
      checking.
- [ ] On the pull-back, robots fly off to the other islands, and those go too.

## 9. Swampman

      and it strikes you, and it's over: straight to the end card, no continuing, whatever walked away afterwards.

## 11. Ship of Theseus: the voyage (from the feedback box; the biggest piece, last)
- [ ] During the sail-away, a toast for a second: "Press S to sail forth". Press it and there's no end card; you take the
      helm with the arrow keys.
- [ ] Sailing: a steady wind from one direction; the boat is fastest across it and can't sail into it, so you tack.
- [ ] Islands with docks:
  - The lighthouse, and its keeper (the Utility Monster's model). He talks, then fights: a simple sword fight, three
        hearts each. Beat him, and a cellar door leads to a chest of gold you can carry back to the ship.
  - The Sentinelese island. They are real, uncontacted, and want to be left alone, so the game respects that: they
        warn you off from the treeline. You can leave gifts (Jolly Ranchers) on the beach and go; you can't go further.
  - An island of frogs; one or two empty ones.
  - An island with the thought-experiment house on it. Go in, and you're back home, and the gold with you (only this
        way).
- [ ] **The museum:** a new room of the house, filled with the special things you've found (the chest of gold first).
